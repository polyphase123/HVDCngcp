Leyte–Luzon HVDC Interconnection Project

The pioneer high-voltage direct current link in the Philippines, linking the Tongonan geothermal power fields in Leyte to the Luzon load center in Metro Manila via a 440 MW, ±350 kV DC transmission corridor.

DC Voltage ±350 kV
Capacity 440 MW
Submarine Span 21 km
Route & Cable Spans
  • • Overhead DC Transmission Line: 430 km of steel lattice towers traversing Camarines Sur, Albay, Sorsogon (Luzon) and Samar to Leyte.
  • • San Bernardino Strait Submarine Cable: 21 km subsea crossing between Matnog (Sorsogon) and Cabacungan/San Isidro (Samar) with high tidal currents (up to 8 knots).
  • • Double-Armor Cable Protection: Specially armored against strong underwater shear forces and maritime anchors.
Converter Stations
  • • Naga Converter Station (Luzon): Located in Camarines Sur. Inverts DC back to 230 kV AC for transmission up to Tayabas and Metro Manila.
  • • Ormoc Converter Station (Visayas): Located in Leyte adjacent to Tongonan Geothermal Power Plant. Converts 230 kV AC to ±350 kV DC.
  • • 12-Pulse Thyristor Valve Bridges: Water-cooled thyristor stacks requiring massive AC harmonic filter yards (11th, 13th, High-pass).
Electrode Return System
  • • Sea/Ground Electrodes: Specialized graphite/magnetite anode and cathode electrode beds in San Jose (Luzon) and Albuera (Leyte).
  • • Monopolar Ground Return Mode: If one pole suffers a line fault or maintenance, the link transfers 220 MW (50% power) continuously via sea return.
  • • Metallic Return Capability: Uses dedicated neutral conductors to eliminate ground currents in sensitive ecological areas.

Strategic Role & Grid Impact

Before this interconnection, Leyte's abundant 700+ MW geothermal energy was stranded locally on a small island grid. The Leyte-Luzon HVDC allowed the National Power Corporation (NPC) and now NGCP to transmit clean baseload renewable energy directly into Metro Manila, reducing Luzon's fossil fuel dependence.

Crucially, the HVDC link acted as an electrical firewall during catastrophic events like Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan) in 2013 and major earthquake events, allowing Luzon's grid to remain unaffected even when Visayas transmission was completely incapacitated.

Mindanao–Visayas Interconnection Project (MVIP)

The landmark infrastructure project completing the "One Grid Philippines" vision. Uses advanced Voltage Source Converter (VSC) technology with Modular Multilevel Converters (MMC) across a 92-kilometer deep-water submarine cable in the Bohol Sea.

DC Voltage ±350 kV
Capacity 450 MW (900 MW Exp.)
Submarine Span 92 km
92-km Deep-Water Submarine Crossing
  • • Subsea Cable Route: Traverses from Santander (southern tip of Cebu) across the Bohol Sea / Mindanao Sea to Dapitan City (Zamboanga del Norte).
  • • Massive Water Depths: Cable reaches ocean depths of up to 650 meters with advanced fiber-optic monitoring cores.
  • • ±350 kV XLPE DC Cable: Solid extruded cross-linked polyethylene dielectric with zero environmental oil leakage risk.
Dumanjug & Lala VSC Converter Stations
  • • Dumanjug CS (Cebu): Interfaced with Cebu 230 kV AC backbone to channel power to/from the Central Visayas industrial hub.
  • • Lala CS (Lanao del Norte): Connected to Mindanao 230 kV network, balancing Agus-Pulangi hydro generation and baseload coal.
  • • Modular Multilevel Converters (MMC): Uses thousands of IGBT valves to generate near-ideal sine waves without AC harmonic filters.
VSC Technical Advantages
  • • Decoupled P-Q Control: Can inject or absorb up to ±200 MVAR of reactive power dynamically, supporting weak island grid voltages like a STATCOM.
  • • Black-Start Capability: In the event of a total blackout in Mindanao or Cebu, MVIP can energize the entire regional grid from the other side.
  • • Instantaneous Power Reversal: Direction of power flow reverses in milliseconds simply by changing DC current flow without polarity switches.

Market Integration & Energy Security Benefits

1. Sharing of Spinning Reserves Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao can now share ancillary reserves, dramatically lowering the overall cost of generation capacity and preventing forced rotational brownouts.
2. Seasonal Hydro Optimization During rainy season in Mindanao, excess hydropower can be exported north to Cebu and Manila. During dry periods, baseload power flows south to Mindanao.
3. Unified WESM Spot Market Enables full price convergence across the Philippine electricity market, allowing consumer electric coops and distribution utilities to buy the cheapest marginal MW.

Technical Reference & Mathematical Formulations

Fundamental electrical engineering equations governing Philippine transmission line dynamics and HVDC/HVAC trade-offs.

1. Submarine Cable Capacitive Charging Current

In AC cables, the continuous dielectric displacement current per unit length is given by:

$$I_c = \omega \cdot C \cdot \frac{V_{LL}}{\sqrt{3}} = 2\pi f \cdot C \cdot \frac{V_{LL}}{\sqrt{3}} \quad [\text{A/km}]$$

Where $f = 60\text{ Hz}$ in the Philippines, $C \approx 0.22\ \mu\text{F/km}$ for subsea XLPE, and $V_{LL} = 230\text{ kV}$.

2. Total Reactive Power Generation ($Q_c$)

Total 3-phase reactive power generated over cable length $L$:

$$Q_c = \omega \cdot C \cdot V_{LL}^2 \cdot L = 2\pi f C V_{LL}^2 L \quad [\text{MVAR}]$$

Notice that $Q_c$ scales quadratically with voltage ($V^2$) and linearly with distance ($L$).

3. Usable Active Power Derating ($P_{\text{usable}}$)

Due to thermal current limitations ($S_{\text{rated}} = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2}$), the remaining active capacity is:

$$P_{\text{usable}}(L) = \sqrt{S_{\text{rated}}^2 - Q_c(L)^2} = \sqrt{S_{\text{rated}}^2 - \left(\omega C V_{LL}^2 L\right)^2}$$

When $Q_c(L) = S_{\text{rated}}$, the cable is 100% occupied by reactive current, yielding $P_{\text{usable}} = 0\text{ MW}$.

4. Critical Submarine Cable Distance ($L_{\text{crit}}$)

The theoretical maximum distance an AC cable can be operated without midpoint compensation:

$$L_{\text{crit}} = \frac{S_{\text{rated}}}{\omega \cdot C \cdot V_{LL}^2} = \frac{S_{\text{rated}}}{2\pi f C V_{LL}^2}$$

For a 500 MVA, 230 kV cable at 60 Hz, $L_{\text{crit}} \approx 125\text{ km}$. However, voltage regulation issues (Ferranti effect) become critical beyond 40 km!
